About

The ideas are mine too.

Nobody handed me a spec for any of this. I notice a problem, decide it should exist as software, then do every part of getting it there: the product thinking, the interface, the architecture, the code, the tests, the store submission. YardLink Studio LLC is the company I founded around that habit. Part studio, part product laboratory.

The range is deliberate, because the problems were not similar. A Caribbean restaurant app needed curation and cultural knowledge. A security job board needed trustworthy pay and licence data. An online racer needed two players to agree on the same race. A sales system needed to refuse to lie. None of those are the same engineering problem, and none of them were assigned to me.

So the work spans AI systems, iOS and Android apps, web platforms, marketplaces, data pipelines, real time multiplayer, internal business tools, and automation. What connects them is not a language or a framework. It is that each one had to actually work, be affordable to run, and be something I could explain line by line.

A pattern shows up across all of it: run the models locally where possible, verify things deterministically instead of asking an AI to grade itself, and keep a human in the loop wherever the cost of being wrong is real.

Engineering philosophy

  • Build the idea. Talking about it is not the same as shipping it.
  • Use AI where it genuinely helps, not as decoration.
  • Keep systems understandable. If I cannot explain it, it is not done.
  • Control costs. Local models and free tiers beat a surprise invoice.
  • Verify before claiming. Deterministic checks over model self grading.
  • Keep humans in the loop when being wrong actually costs something.
  • Never fake an integration. A typed handoff beats a pretend API.
  • Turn experiments into working software, not screenshots.

AI Sales Intelligence

Winston

YardLink Studio's internal AI sales intelligence and outreach operating system.

Internal tool · Active development
Prospect→Research→Identify problems→ Map to offerings→Price→Write→ Guardian→Human approval→Send→Track
Internal system for YardLink Studio

Winston is the system that finds YardLink Studio its customers. It looks up a local business, works out what is actually wrong with their digital presence, decides whether YardLink can genuinely help, prices the work, writes the email, checks that email against the evidence, and then waits for a human to approve it before anything is sent.

It is not an email bot. An email bot sends a template to a list. Winston has to be able to answer "why are we contacting this business, and what exactly are we offering them," and it has to be right, because the message goes out with the company name on it.

What I built

The entire system: the research pipeline, the problem detection with evidence and confidence scoring, the catalogue that knows what the company can legitimately sell, the pricing model, the drafting layer, the validation layer, the approval queue, and the inbox handling for replies and bounces.

The work is split into modules with separate responsibilities, because each stage has a different contract: Scout finds prospects, Researcher investigates, Auditor identifies problems, Strategist picks the offer, Pricer estimates scope, Writer drafts, Guardian validates, and Inbox classifies replies. Negotiator and Learner are designed but not yet built. These are functions with defined inputs and outputs, not separate language models wearing name badges.

The interesting engineering

  • Guardian can veto, and it never uses AI to do it. Every check is pattern matching against stored evidence. Asking one model whether another model made something up is circular reasoning, and it costs money to get an unreliable answer. Deterministic rules are reproducible, explainable, and free.
  • Nothing gets sent without a person. A blocked draft is saved for inspection but never reaches the approval queue, because a message that should not be sent should not be one click away from going out.
  • The catalogue knows proof from product. Some projects demonstrate capability and some are actually for sale. Winston enforces that difference, so it can never email a restaurant offering to sell them an internal tool.

Running it for almost nothing

Everything runs on local models. Simple work like classifying a reply goes to a small 3B model, and harder work like drafting or auditing goes to an 8B model. Routing by difficulty means the system stays sharp where it matters and cheap everywhere else.

A paid provider is unreachable unless it is explicitly switched on, so a free service failing never quietly authorizes spending money. A stronger model also cannot bypass Guardian, because which model wrote the text has nothing to do with whether the text is allowed to be sent.

How it connects to the Website Builder

When Winston finds a business that needs a website, it produces a structured handoff shaped to the Builder's real data format, which an operator then imports. This is deliberately a documented handoff and not an API integration, because the Builder does not expose a suitable HTTP API. Inventing one would have meant faking a connection that does not exist.

Website Fulfillment System

YardLink Studio Website Builder

The website production system behind YardLink Studio's fulfillment side.

Internal tool · Active development
Structured intake→Content and assets→Generation→ Editing→Audit→Review→Publish
Internal system for YardLink Studio

When YardLink Studio takes on a client who needs a website, this is what builds it. You answer questions about the business once, and it produces a finished, responsive website plus a matching set of logos. The point is not to replace a designer. The point is that the tenth client site should not cost the same effort as the first.

Most website builders hand you a blank template and leave the design work to you. This one works the other way around: structured information about a business goes in, and a complete site comes out, ready for a human to refine.

What I built

The full production workflow: an eight step intake, the site generation engine, the theme and layout system, document and image ingestion, the AI copywriting layer, a deterministic quality auditor, client and project management, and the publishing pipeline.

It reads the documents an owner actually has rather than demanding clean data. A PDF menu, a Word document, or a photograph of a printed menu all work, the last one through on device text recognition. Sections are composed by industry, so a restaurant gets a menu and a barbershop gets a booking prompt instead of an empty menu section.

The interesting engineering

  • The AI writes words, never markup. The model produces taglines, hero copy, and descriptions. It never emits HTML. Generation stays a pure function from data to string, which keeps output reproducible and testable rather than surprising.
  • Layout and theme are genuinely independent. Ten themes, six typeface pairings, and eight layout families combine so the underlying page structure and section order actually change, rather than recoloring the same template.
  • The whole core path runs in the browser. No backend, and client data never leaves the device. Cloud features are credential gated and off by default.
  • Exported sites carry their own images. Processed photos are embedded directly, so the downloaded site works anywhere without a hosting dependency.
  • A deterministic auditor scores every site on content completeness, SEO, accessibility, and technical issues before anyone publishes.

Deliberate limits

The Builder supports exactly eight industries: Restaurant, Barber and Beauty, Church, Law Firm, Medical, Real Estate, Retail, and Other. Anything outside that list uses the Other structure. A security company, for example, would fall under Other rather than getting a bespoke template.

It also emits exactly seven section types: about, menu, gallery, team, accolades, call to action, and contact. Those boundaries are enforced in code rather than left to judgement, which is what stops the sales side from promising something the production side cannot make.

Publishing, described honestly

There is real publishing infrastructure: a state machine and a Cloudflare Worker backed by key value storage. It is not managed hosting. The operator deploys that worker to their own Cloudflare account, creates the namespace, and supplies the URL and token. Sites can also simply be downloaded and hosted anywhere.

Restaurant Discovery

YardLink Eats

Caribbean restaurant discovery for NYC, Long Island, and Westchester.

Live on the App Store
For anyone looking for Caribbean food in New York

YardLink Eats helps people find Caribbean restaurants across New York City, Long Island, and Westchester. Jamaican, Guyanese, Haitian, and Trinidadian food, with real photographs, the dishes worth ordering, how far away each place is, and a guide called Errol who can actually talk about the food.

The reason it exists is simple: there was no good way to find this food. Google Maps buries it under chains, and Yelp does not understand the difference between a roti shop in Richmond Hill and a jerk spot in the Bronx. This is the app I wanted to exist, so I built it. It is live on the App Store with 694 curated restaurants.

What I built

All of it, solo: the iOS app, the Android app, the web experience, the AI assistant backend, and the data pipeline that decides which restaurants are good enough to ship. Four separate pieces of engineering, each with its own stack.

The four pieces

  • iOS. Swift and SwiftUI, with SwiftData for local storage, MapKit for the map, and CoreLocation for GPS distance to each restaurant.
  • Android. Kotlin with the Gradle Kotlin DSL, built to match the iOS feature set rather than lag behind it.
  • Web. Plain JavaScript, no framework. The site exists to explain the product and send people to the app, so it did not need one.
  • Backend. A Netlify Function serving Errol, with Firebase Firestore for restaurant data and Firebase Storage for photographs.

The interesting engineering

  • Errol runs on Groq with Llama 3.3 70B. It previously ran through an Anthropic compatible interface. Rather than update two mobile apps, I wrote the new backend to speak the exact same request and response format. The model provider changed underneath and neither app needed a single line changed. That is what a stable contract buys you.
  • Photographs are bought once and owned forever. Images come from Google Places one time, then live in Firebase Storage permanently. The app never re-fetches and never re-bills. This started as a worry about the bill and ended up being the better architecture, because the product no longer depends on an external image service staying available or affordable.
  • The API key never ships inside the app. It stays server side in the function, where a decompiled binary cannot reach it.
  • Every listing is reviewed by a human before it reaches a user. That review system became its own project, Anansi.

Security Job Marketplace

GuardLink

Active dev
For security guards looking for work

GuardLink is a job marketplace built specifically for security professionals. It helps guards find security work across New York City, Long Island, and Westchester, and it puts the things that actually decide whether a job is worth applying to right on the card: the pay, the hours, the shift, and which licenses you need.

Most job boards bury that information three paragraphs into a description. A guard deciding between two overnight shifts should not have to dig for the hourly rate.

What I built

The marketplace itself, the job discovery engine that pulls openings from employer hiring systems, authentication with three separate roles for guards, employers, and admins, saved jobs and profiles, licence tracking, resume upload, the employer posting and review workflow, and a mobile client.

The discovery engine only reads structured sources: public applicant tracking APIs from Greenhouse and Lever, and schema.org job data published on company career pages. It deliberately never scrapes raw HTML. That is a correctness decision rather than a missing feature, because guessing a job title and a wage out of unstructured markup produces listings that look real and are wrong, and a wrong wage is worse for a guard than no listing at all. The mobile app is a thin client of this platform, with no second database and no duplicate business rules.

Listings shown are seeded sample data. The marketplace, discovery engine, and workflows are real; employer boards are not yet connected.

Data & Editorial Engine

Anansi

Internal tool
The editorial desk behind YardLink Eats

Anansi is the system that decides which restaurants are good enough to appear in YardLink Eats. It gathers restaurant information, cleans it up, works out what is missing, and then hands a human editor a workspace to make the final call on every single listing.

It is not a scraper. A scraper collects data. Anansi collects data and then argues with you about it, proposing badges and list placements with a confidence score and its reasoning, which a person then approves or rejects.

What I built

The collection pipeline, the classification and enrichment steps, the editorial review interface with its queues for pending, approved, missing photos and missing dishes, the suggestion engine with confidence scoring, and a question answering layer that cites its sources using a local model, so asking costs nothing.

This is the clearest human in the loop AI system I have built. The model never publishes anything. It queues a recommendation, shows the evidence behind it, and a person decides. Roughly 751 restaurants sit in the pipeline, and 694 have passed review and shipped to the app. Those two numbers being different is the entire point of the system.

Real-Time Multiplayer Game

Avenue Run

Active dev
For anyone who wants to race a friend

Avenue Run is a neon side scrolling endless runner you can play solo or race against one other person online. You pick a character, you run, you dodge, and you try to beat the person in the other browser.

What I built

The game itself in the Phaser engine, including movement, obstacles, the jetpack mechanic, scoring, combos, run missions, and character customisation. Then the multiplayer server, which is the part I would actually want to talk about.

Two players racing need to agree on what is happening, and that is genuinely hard once the players are on different machines. The server gives each race room exactly one authoritative owner using a Cloudflare Durable Object, keyed by the room code. Every WebSocket connection for a given code is routed to the one object that owns that room, so there is never a disagreement about who is winning. Durable Objects exist precisely to give a distributed system one place where state is real, and a race is exactly that shape of problem. The wire protocol between client and server is tested separately from the game.

Wellness Mobile App

Otonia

In store review
For people dealing with stress and racing thoughts

Otonia is a wellness app for the ordinary bad evenings: overthinking, anxiety, not being able to wind down. It combines an AI companion you can talk to, a journal, a daily check in, and small games that are genuinely fun rather than clinical, all inside a calm persistent world that learns what actually helps you. You pick a companion who stays with you, check in on how you are doing, and choose how you want to be helped: play something, be distracted, talk it out, calm down, or write it out.

It is not a therapist and makes no medical claims. It is meant to be the thing you open at eleven at night when your brain will not stop.

What I built

The full mobile app for both iOS and Android: the companion, the journal, the check in loop, the mini games with real audio and rendering, the personalisation engine, authentication, and the store submission work for both platforms.

Accessibility was treated as an engineering requirement, not a checkbox. Reduced motion is respected throughout, and no game encodes meaning in colour alone, which matters when a stressed or colourblind user is the entire audience. It carries the largest test suite of anything I have built.

Wedding Planning & Guest Experience

WedLink

Active dev
For couples planning a wedding and their guests

WedLink lets a couple set up their wedding, invite guests individually or by a shared link, and see who is actually coming. Guests open their invitation with a link or a short code and reply without ever creating an account, because nobody wants to make a password to tell their cousin they will be at the reception.

It is a wedding planning app, not just an RSVP form. The couple gets one place for the wedding details, the invitation, the guest list, seating, the budget and expenses, and every reply as it comes in. Meal counts and dietary needs add themselves up as guests answer, so the caterer question answers itself. The guest side is translated into eight languages, since the people invited to a wedding do not all read the same one.

What I built

The mobile app for both platforms, the guest invitation and RSVP flow, the organiser dashboard, guest and meal tracking, invitation sharing through short codes and deep links, guest list and seating, budget and expense tracking, invitation styling, guest video uploads, localisation into eight languages, and the entire database security model.

The interesting work is in the backend. Letting anonymous guests read and write specific wedding data without letting them see anyone else's wedding is a real access control problem. The result is 33 migrations across 27 tables, row level security on every one of them, and 32 policies, verified by a thirty step synthetic run using genuinely separate organiser and guest sessions. Monetary gifts are deliberately switched off. Handling other people's wedding money is a responsibility I did not want to take on without being certain.
